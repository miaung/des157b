Project Overview

My topic was on privacy, and my target audience was young adults who are used to personalized experiences that rely on their data. My goal was to inform them on laws regarding privacy by highlighting current policies and how a possible future can impact how their data is protected.

The features I initially envisioned for my project were a way to assess the strength of your password compared to others' and to evaluate your data protection level with others. However, my vision shifted from cybersecurity to privacy laws after researching and understanding what laws exist regarding citizens' privacy, and to what extent their privacy is protected. This led me to create a possible future where your data is monetized and how choices related to that can impact your privacy.

Project Research

For UX Research, I did a comparative analysis of two websites, The Data Detox Kit and User Inyerface. The Data Detox Kit provides users with step-by-step instructions on how to protect their online presence and data privacy. The website provides detailed information on which apps are taking information from you and what information they are collecting. This website gave me insight into what data websites were taking from users, and also influenced me to look up current information on privacy laws.

The purpose of User Inyerface is to provide a very bad user experience. It purposefully goes against industry-standard guidelines for websites, creating a very frustrating and almost emotionally draining experience for the user. This project helped me understand the importance of UI design and how it can impact users' emotions, and also how time-consuming creating an effective password can be.

Overall, the websites gave me information on how data is currently collected from users, and how important current security measures are for passwords and creating accounts. The research inspired me to understand just how much data is collected from users and if they have a say in what can be collected.

Visual Design

When I initially planned out the visual design, I knew I wanted to create an ominous feeling for the users. The reason for this is that the project topic was related to data tracking, something that impacts all of our data and how it can be tied back to us. It wouldn't feel appropriate to have a fun and inviting design, because this topic is serious and is not talked about often.

To execute this design vision, I wanted to make the overall design dark and mysterious. That's why I chose a black and white color palette and a sans-serif font. The focus is on the content itself, rather than the design. To further emphasize the ominous theme, I wanted to make sure the opening statement piqued the reader's interest. The site begins with “Your data is protected,” then scrolls to “Your data is protected… kind of.” It instills a false sense of security within users, then retracts that statement.

User Interface

For the UI design, I wanted to make sure that users only focused on the introductory text. As the user first loads the page, there is nothing else for them to do, which prompts them to scroll and read the rest of the text. Once the user scrolls down to the bottom of the page, they are met with a slider. When it came to designing this page, I initially wanted to create an overlay above the slider that displays the possible outcome in a future where data is monetized. Because this was the most visually interesting part of the page, I tried to come up with different designs to execute.

One of the designs I wanted to do was a casino slot machine that randomizes the chances of your risks and benefits by using a jQuery Bandit plugin. However, this proved to be difficult as it was hard for me to figure out the functionality of the slot chances in the backend. If I were given more time, I would like to work on this animation and functionality further.

As an alternative, I chose to use a slider that shows what your future could look like depending on how much data you want to share. This allows the user to explore different privacy choices by visualizing what that potential future could look like. This slider was easy to execute and effectively conveyed the message of how privacy could be monitored in the future.

Revisions

For my usability testing, I wanted to see if my introductory text had the impact that I wanted. To do this, I let the user explore the page with only a pop-up prompt that stated, “You are a young adult who has just graduated from college. As you begin job searching and caring about your online presence, you become curious about how much of your data is out there for the world to see. You start browsing websites on current privacy laws and come across this site that explores a possible future with unusual privacy protections. Read through this website and see what information you can learn through your experience. Scroll to continue reading.” The user was able to explore the page by scrolling without being prompted to. After the user was done scrolling through the beginning, they stated that the design palette felt appropriate to the “ominous” theme of the topic.

As for revisions, my user commented that there was a lot of scrolling in order to get through the text. I implemented this by adjusting the viewpoint height for each line of text, allowing the user to get through the text without having to scroll a lot. There was also a black screen with no text when the user first loaded in, so I got rid of that by decreasing the top margin. If I could further develop this project, I would add a typewriter effect to the text, so it feels as if someone is typing to the user, and I would add some moving gradients to the background so that the design isn’t too boring.

Summary

I learned how to use GSAP for the first time by working on this project. I used it for the scroll trigger animations for the text. The tutorials were pretty straightforward, and I was glad to implement a library that just became widely available to everyone.

I'm most proud of the topic and the quality with which I presented it. A lot of people raised good questions and comments about how data is currently being traced to users, and what that could end up looking like in the future. I feel like my message was strong and did what it was supposed to.

The most challenging part was figuring out the slider visuals. A lot of designs that I initially wanted didn't make it to the final design, but I'm proud of what I was able to complete on such short notice. I hope to further improve this project in the future.
